Internal Assessment (IA)

IB Guide Pages 51-58

Business research project

Duration: 20 hours

Weighting: 30% SL and 20% HL

Toolkits that could be used

Supporting Documents - Audio/Video rules - https://docs.google.com/document/d/15sKjPW2Mv_WS6pMxkae1NSSJ6hN-6MaE/edit

Hence, Essentially - Characteristics of a strong RQ:

  1. Focused and specific
    • It should target one clear decision or issue faced by the business.
    • Avoid being too broad (e.g., “How can Starbucks improve profitability?” is too vague).
    • Instead: “Should Starbucks introduce plant-based milk alternatives in Mexico City stores to increase revenue?”
  2. Decision-oriented
    • The RQ should help a manager/owner make a decision.
    • Good phrasing: “Should…”, “To what extent should…”, “Which option would be more effective…”.
  3. Linked to the syllabus
    • Must allow you to apply BM concepts, tools, and theories (SWOT, Ansoff, decision trees, ratios, etc.).
    • Examiners want to see that your analysis is rooted in IB BM theory, not just description.
  4. Feasible with primary + secondary data
    • You need to gather information (interviews, surveys, observations, financial data, etc.).
    • If you can’t realistically collect the evidence, it won’t work.
  5. Context-specific
    • Tie it to a real business with a real issue.
    • Avoid generic “school project” questions.

Weak RQ examples:

  • “How can Coca-Cola improve marketing?” (too broad, no clear decision)
  • “What is the best strategy for Tesla in the future?” (too speculative, vague)

Strong RQ examples:

  • “Should a local bakery in Monterrey introduce online delivery to increase sales revenue?”
  • “To what extent should Gym X raise its membership fees to improve profitability?”
  • “Which market entry method should Brand Y use to expand into Guatemala — franchising or joint venture?”

A quick test: A good RQ will make you think: If I were the manager, would this question help me decide what to do next?
